## Slide copy

1. You have thoughtful testimonials—and the decision still stalls in the same place. Your proof may be answering the wrong doubt.
2. Proof is not decoration beside a promise. Its job is to reduce the uncertainty standing closest to the next step.
3. Match proof to the hesitation: fit needs a relevant example; process needs plain steps; trust needs specific, checkable evidence.
4. Put proof where the doubt appears: identify the claim, the doubt and the detail that resolves it.
5. Before choosing proof, ask: What doubt could stop someone here—and does this proof answer it? Save this before choosing your next proof point.
